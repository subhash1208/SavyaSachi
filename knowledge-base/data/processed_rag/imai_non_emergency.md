# General Triage Protocol: Clinical Reasoning & Abnormalities
**Source:** WHO IMAI (Integrated Management of Adolescent and Adult Illness) - Vol 1
**Condition:** Adult Non-Emergency Assessment

## Clinical Reasoning & Triage
* **Step 1:** Always triage for severe conditions and conditions that could potentially deteriorate quickly using the Quick Check. Immediately provide emergency treatment and perform emergency laboratory investigations before further assessment.
* **Step 2:** Ascertain the likely cause of each presenting symptom by gathering available evidence (history, physical examination, further investigations). Consider patient demographics, risk factors, negative findings, combinations of signs/symptoms, and prior interventions.
* **Step 3:** Formulate a differential diagnosis. Avoid premature closure of the diagnostic process. Prioritize life-threatening causes ("What could kill a patient quickly"), followed by common endemic/epidemic causes ("Sutton's Law").

## Management of Electrolyte Abnormalities

### 1. Sodium (Na) Abnormalities
* **Hypernatremia (Na > 145 mmol/L):** Usually caused by a relative deficit of free water (GI losses, sweat, renal losses, insufficient intake) rather than excess sodium. 
  * *Treatment:* Avoid rapid correction (risk of cerebral edema). Replace volume using Oral Rehydration or 0.9% Normal Saline until hemodynamically stable, then switch to 5% Dextrose. Lower Na by max 10 mmol/L in the first 24 hrs.
* **Hyponatremia (Na < 130 mmol/L):** Classified by volume status (Hypovolemic, Euvolemic, Hypervolemic).
  * *Treatment:* Cautiously hydrate hypovolemic patients with Normal Saline. Restrict fluids and consider loop diuretics in euvolemic/hypervolemic patients. Correct max 10 mmol/L in first 24 hrs, max 18 mmol/L in 48 hrs. *(Emergency seizures/coma: query Drug DB for hypertonic saline rates).*

### 2. Potassium (K) Abnormalities
* **Hyperkalemia (K > 5.5 mmol/L):** Asymptomatic until severe arrhythmias occur.
  * *Treatment (Severe):* Give IV calcium gluconate to stabilize cardiac membrane if ECG changes are present. Use short-acting insulin in D50 and Salbutamol to activate intracellular transfer of K. Check Drug DB for exact dosages.
* **Hypokalemia (K < 3.5 mmol/L):**
  * *Treatment:* Oral potassium supplements (10-20 mmol every 6-12 hours) or K-rich foods for mild cases. Severe cases require careful IV potassium in saline. *NEVER give a bolus dose of intravenous K*.

### 3. Calcium (Ca) Abnormalities
* **Hypercalcemia (> 2.65 mmol/L):** 
  * *Treatment:* Urgent rehydration with 0.9% Normal Saline aiming for high urine output. Once hydrated, loop diuretics (furosemide) can be used. Consider steroids (prednisolone) for specific etiologies like lymphomas/sarcoidosis.
